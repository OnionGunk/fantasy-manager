What the words mean

Starting
These players earn you points this week. Bench players earn nothing, no matter how well they play.
Bench
Players you own who are not playing for you this week. They are insurance, and you can swap them into your lineup any time before their game starts.
Flex
A lineup slot that accepts a running back, a wide receiver, or a tight end. It exists so you can start whoever is playing best that week, regardless of position.
Bye week
One week each season where a player's real NFL team does not play at all. A player on a bye scores exactly zero. Starting one by accident is the single most common way to lose a week.
Waivers
The queue for claiming players nobody owns. Instead of first-come first-served, claims are collected and processed all at once on a set day each week, so everyone gets a fair shot at a player.
Free agent
A player no team in your league owns. After the waiver period passes, anyone can add them instantly.
OUT / Doubtful / Questionable
Official NFL injury labels. OUT means they will definitely not play, so they score zero. Doubtful means they probably won't. Questionable is a genuine coin flip, usually decided about 90 minutes before kickoff.
IR (injured reserve)
A player hurt badly enough to be out for several weeks at minimum. Your league gives you one spare slot to park a player like that in, and it does not count against your normal roster. Never drop someone sitting in it — the slot is free.
FAAB budget
Your league uses blind bidding for waiver claims. You get $100 for the whole season, and when you want an unowned player you say what you would pay. Highest bid wins and the money is gone. It is not real money. Spending nothing all season is the same as wasting it.
Projected points
How many points analysts expect a player to score this week. Useful for comparing two players. Not a promise.
